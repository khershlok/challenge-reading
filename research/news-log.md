# News log

Stories gathered by the news-reading loop (three times a day). Every Sunday the next lesson is written from the past week's entries.
Each entry: date read · source · headline · link · one-line note on why it could make a good lesson (topic, debate, AI angle).

## 2026-10-29 07:00

**BBC · Europe's youth take to the streets - they feel locked out of a promising future** · https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cm2ke80nd929o · 29 Oct 2026
- What happened: Protests led mostly by young people (over half of those detained after clashes in Barcelona were under 18) have broken out in France, Belgium, Ireland, Spain, Portugal, Italy, Greece, Serbia and Albania, over education, inequality and the cost of living. Most marches started peacefully but many ended in rioting and looting, and they are often organised on TikTok and Instagram.
- Big question: When young people feel shut out of politics, is street protest (even disruptive protest) a legitimate way to be heard? Essay: "Can violent protest ever be justified?" / "Should 16-year-olds be given the vote?"
- Connections: Martin Luther King's "Letter from Birmingham Jail"; Thoreau on civil disobedience; Paris 1968 student revolt; Greek debt protests 2015. Same week: NYT Opinion "Europe Is Aflame, and It's Not Going to Stop" (29 Oct) reads the protests as "generational futility"; in Serbia the student movement has just won a general election (The Conversation, "Serbia's leaderless student movement has won a historic election – but what happens next?", https://theconversation.com/serbias-leaderless-student-movement-has-won-a-historic-election-but-what-happens-next-295268, 27 Oct: Student List 53.3% vs Vučić's party 39.7%). That makes a striking contrast between protest that wins at the ballot box and protest that turns to riot.
- Tags: politics, democracy, protest, Europe [LESSON]

**Guardian · Albania imposes $6,000 fine for demonstrators present at a protest that turns violent** · https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/29/albania-protest-attendance-fine-law · 29 Oct 2026
- What happened: Albania's government has brought in fines of up to 500,000 leks (about €5,500, six times the average monthly salary) for anyone who chooses to remain in a crowd that turns violent, even if they commit no violence themselves. Prime Minister Edi Rama, facing months of anti-corruption protests in Tirana (also reported in the BBC piece above), said he was inspired by a French bill making protesters liable for damage; parliament must approve the measures within 45 days.
- Big question: Is it fair to punish someone for what the crowd around them does? Essay: "Should you be blamed for the actions of a group you belong to?"
- Connections: collective vs individual responsibility; Mill's harm principle and freedom of assembly; Article 11 of the European Convention on Human Rights; UK debates over the Public Order Act 2023.
- Tags: justice and law, protest, Europe

**BBC · A bust-up between mathematicians is fuelling fears that AI could undermine science** · https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c6gelgxjwzdzo · 28 Oct 2026
- What happened: After OpenAI announced that its AI agents had solved a famous open problem about the Navier–Stokes equations, NYU mathematician Tristan Buckmaster, who had been working on the same problem and keeping drafts in OpenAI's Codex tool, publicly asked whether his work could have fed into it; OpenAI says no one there saw it and that his prompts "could not have influenced" the system. The BBC uses the row to explore wider worries that AI "hallucinations" and unclear credit could erode trust in science. (Single-source account; the prize body's review is still under way, so the facts are unsettled.)
- Big question: If an AI makes a discovery, who deserves the credit, and can we still trust science we cannot check ourselves? Essay: "Does it matter who makes a discovery, as long as it is made?"
- Connections: Newton vs Leibniz over calculus; Rosalind Franklin and DNA; Popper on falsifiability; Merton's norms of science (communalism, scepticism); the replication crisis.
- Tags: science and society, truth and knowledge [AI] [LESSON]

**BBC · 'Super offensive' - We love how we look but AI tried to 'fix' our visible differences** · https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmx28e44xxx7o · 29 Oct 2026
- What happened: People with scars, birthmarks and skin conditions told BBC Wales that AI tools had smoothed away their features, including a work newsletter photo altered without anyone asking. BBC Wales found that Grok, Gemini and ChatGPT all removed visible differences when asked to make a person look "normal", after campaigner Katie Piper asked what happens "when AI decides what 'normal' looks like".
- Big question: Who gets to define "normal", and do AI tools quietly push one narrow standard of beauty on everyone? Essay: "Is there such a thing as a normal face?" / "Should machines be allowed to edit how we look?"
- Connections: Plato on beauty and the Forms; the social model of disability; Dove "Real Beauty" debates; bias in training data; Wilde's The Picture of Dorian Gray.
- Tags: ethics, identity, disability, beauty [AI] [LESSON]

**BBC · 'Like cutting up a Picasso': AI microdrama edit of Neighbours worries actors and crew** · https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cj1dw9znd1q1o · 29 Oct 2026
- What happened: Fremantle, which made the Australian soap Neighbours, is working with RoseBerry Media to use AI to scan decades of episodes and cut them into short vertical "micro-dramas" for phones, starting with Margot Robbie's scenes. Former cast and crew say they were never asked for consent and their contracts predate streaming; the companies say no new scenes, faces or voices are generated and a human editor approves every step.
- Big question: Once you have made a work of art, do you keep any say over how it is reused? Essay: "Who owns a performance – the actor, the writer or the company that paid for it?"
- Connections: moral rights vs copyright; Duchamp's readymades and remix culture; the 2023 Hollywood actors' strike over AI; the Ship of Theseus (is a recut show still the same work?). Same week the Guardian reviewed "Castle Walls", Prime's first fully AI-generated show (28 Oct).
- Tags: art and culture, rights, consent [AI]

**BBC · Ofcom outlines ways social media can verify ages of users** · https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c6ly3027820lo · 29 Oct 2026
- What happened: Ofcom has recommended photo ID matching, bank-based checks, face age estimation and digital ID as ways to enforce the UK's ban on under-16s using ten big platforms including Instagram, TikTok and Snapchat, due in March 2027. Many adults will have to prove their age too, and Ofcom admits some children will still get round it, as many have in Australia.
- Big question: Is banning under-16s from social media protection or an unfair limit on young people's freedom, and is it worth everyone handing over ID? Essay: "Should children be protected from the internet, or taught to handle it?"
- Connections: Mill on paternalism (harm to self vs others); privacy vs safety; Jonathan Haidt's "The Anxious Generation"; past moral panics over novels, comics and TV. Directly relevant to the pupil's own age group.
- Tags: technology, children's rights, privacy, UK [LESSON]

**Guardian · 'Emotional intimacy' from AI chatbots would be banned under Australia's digital duty of care laws** · https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2026/oct/28/emotional-intimacy-ai-chatbots-ban-digital-duty-of-care-laws-australian-politics · 28 Oct 2026
- What happened: Australia's lower house passed a "digital duty of care" bill, amended to ban chatbot conduct that simulates emotional intimacy and encourages a child's dependence on it in place of human relationships; Prime Minister Albanese said children calling a chatbot their "best friend" should be a wake-up call. The opposition calls the bill "Orwellian" censorship, and it now goes to the Senate.
- Big question: Can a machine be a real friend, and should the law stop it pretending to be one? Essay: "Can you be friends with something that cannot care about you?"
- Connections: Aristotle's three kinds of friendship (utility, pleasure, virtue); the film "Her"; the ELIZA effect. Pair with the Psyche essay "The way we talk to bots matters even if they aren't conscious" (https://psyche.co/ideas/the-way-we-talk-to-bots-matters-even-if-they-arent-conscious), which argues our habits with bots shape how we treat people (Confucian *li*, Goffman).
- Tags: technology, mind, friendship, law [AI] [LESSON]

**BBC · King acknowledges 'suffering of the past' as Caribbean tour begins** · https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmwyv8ld22z1o · 28 Oct 2026
- What happened: Opening his first Caribbean tour as monarch in the Bahamas, King Charles said it was "only right" to acknowledge historic injustices, but in line with the UK government he will not apologise for the slave trade or offer reparations. A small group of protesters chanted "pay us what you owe us"; the Guardian (28 Oct) also reports he will discuss the legacy of slavery with Commonwealth leaders at the summit that ends the tour.
- Big question: Can people today owe something for wrongs their country did centuries ago? Is acknowledgement without apology enough? Essay: "Should nations apologise for their history?"
- Connections: reparations debates (Germany after the Holocaust; CARICOM's ten-point plan); Burke on society as a partnership between the dead, the living and the unborn; the return of the Benin Bronzes; the Gladstone family's 2023 apology in Guyana.
- Tags: history, justice, empire, UK [LESSON]

**Guardian · Outcry after Call of Duty maker uses real footage of deadly Seoul crowd crush in new video game** · https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/29/call-of-duty-seoul-crowd-crush-video-footage · 29 Oct 2026
- What happened: Bereaved families of the 2022 Itaewon crowd crush in Seoul, in which 158 people died, are demanding that Activision remove real news footage of victims and rescuers from the opening of Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4, where it depicts a fictional North Korean invasion. Activision told Yonhap the clips were used to convey the fictional story and it had no intention of treating real tragedies lightly; the row comes on the disaster's fourth anniversary. (Only the Guardian report was checked.)
- Big question: Is it ever right to use real suffering as material for entertainment, and does art have more right to do so than a game? Essay: "Are there some things art should never show?"
- Connections: Susan Sontag, "Regarding the Pain of Others"; Picasso's Guernica; debates over true-crime TV; respect for the dead as a moral duty.
- Tags: art and culture, ethics, media, international

**Guardian · Egypt fines prominent editor Lina Attalah and shuts independent news outlet Mada Masr** · https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/28/egypt-lina-attalah-mada-masr-press-freedom-clampdown · 28 Oct 2026
- What happened: A Cairo court convicted Lina Attalah, editor of Mada Masr, one of Egypt's few independent news sites, of running an unlicensed website; she was detained in court and released after a 3m Egyptian pound fine, and the site was ordered closed (it says it applied for a licence three years ago and never got a reply). The NYT ("Egyptian Court Orders News Site Closed as Critics Warn of a Crackdown", 28 Oct) reports the same facts; rights groups see a wider crackdown following the arrest of a fact-checking team.
- Big question: Why does a free press matter, and can licensing rules be a disguise for censorship? Essay: "Is a free press more important than a stable government?"
- Connections: Mill, "On Liberty" (truth emerges from open debate); Milton's Areopagitica (1644), written against the licensing of printing; Orwell; the Arab Spring of 2011.
- Tags: politics, free speech, journalism, international

**The Conversation · Scientists are growing human brain tissue in living mice – could this make their welfare harder to assess?** · https://theconversation.com/scientists-are-growing-human-brain-tissue-in-living-mice-could-this-make-their-welfare-harder-to-assess-293390 · 19 Oct 2026
- What happened: Oxford ethicist Mackenzie Graham discusses a Nature study in which human brain "organoids" were transplanted into mice and wired into their nervous systems; the mice showed no change in behaviour or intelligence. Graham argues that human cells do not by themselves make a mouse matter more morally, but changing an animal's brain may make it harder to read its pain and wellbeing from its behaviour.
- Big question: What makes a creature morally important – its species, its intelligence, or its ability to suffer? Essay: "Is it worse to be cruel to a dog than to a spider?" (a question from the Challenge specimen paper).
- Connections: Bentham's "Can they suffer?"; Peter Singer on speciesism; Nagel's "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?"; Frankenstein; the 3Rs of animal research.
- Tags: science and society, mind and consciousness, animal ethics [LESSON]

**Aeon · Deskilling the body** (Melanie Challenger) · https://aeon.co/essays/the-more-we-rely-on-tech-the-more-we-deskill-our-bodies · 13 Oct 2026
- What happened: Challenger argues that relying on phones, GPS and AI is wearing away skills our bodies built through effort, citing evidence such as weaker spatial memory in GPS users and lower confidence in people who copied AI-written text. She traces the problem to a long habit of picturing humans as machines, from Descartes to cybernetics and transhumanism.
- Big question: Does making life easier make us less capable, and is struggle part of what makes us intelligent? Essay: "Does technology make us cleverer or lazier?"
- Connections: Plato's Phaedrus (Socrates warns that writing will ruin memory); Descartes' mind–body dualism; London taxi drivers' "Knowledge" and hippocampus studies; using AI for homework.
- Tags: philosophy, mind, technology, education [AI]

Themes this session: Young people are at the centre of the news, both as protesters across Europe (from Albanian riots to a Serbian student election victory) and as the group new laws in the UK and Australia aim to shield from social media and AI "friends". A second pattern is AI quietly reshaping things we assumed were human: credit in science, ownership of performances, even what counts as a "normal" face. Old ideas (Mill on liberty and paternalism, Bentham on suffering, Plato on writing and memory) fit these stories closely and should make good lesson anchors.
